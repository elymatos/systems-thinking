# Glossary

Consolidated definitions of every term introduced across the chapters, alphabetical, each
cross-referenced to the chapter where it's elaborated. Intended as a quick-reference index and as
raw material for the later image-schema grounding phase (see [`README.md`](README.md)).

**Abstraction** — the process of successively removing layers of detail from a representation to
capture only a system's most essential features. → [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Adaptive system** — a system whose course of development is the product of continuously acting
on, and reacting to, feedback loops; complexity increases when two or more adaptive systems react
to each other's behavior over time. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md), [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Agency** — the capacity to make choices directed at an internal goal or value; distinct from
**autonomy**, which is only independence from other systems. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Agent** — an adaptive element of a complex adaptive system, acting on local information only,
with no global view of the whole system. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Allopoiesis** — a system produced by something other than itself, according to a pattern
imposed from outside (e.g. a factory producing tractors). Contrast **Autopoiesis**. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Analysis** — the traditional, reductionist method of reasoning: understand a system by breaking
it into constituent parts, describing each in isolation, then recombining. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Antifragility** — a system's capacity to actually improve as a result of disorder or stress,
rather than merely resisting (robustness) or adapting to (resilience) it. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Atrophy** — the risk a closed system runs by resisting new inputs: it ceases to serve a useful
function in its environment and may be deemed unnecessary. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Attractor** — a state, or set of states, that a dynamical system tends to settle into and
return to after disturbance, regardless of many different starting conditions. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Autonomy** — the degree to which a system can function independently of other systems in its
environment, made possible by its internal integrity. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Autopoiesis** — a system's capacity to continuously reproduce and maintain its own components
and organization from within, preserving its identity despite total turnover of its constituent
matter (e.g. a living cell). Contrast **Allopoiesis**. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Bifurcation** — the point at which a system's trajectory branches into a qualitatively new
attractor rather than returning to its previous one. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Black box** — a system whose internal functioning is unknown or ignored; viewed purely in terms
of its inputs and outputs. → [02](02-system-fundamentals.md)

**Boundary** — the limit demarcating a system's internal components and processes from its
environment; where the nexus of relations enabling autonomous functioning reaches its limit. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Bottom-up causality** — the view that a system's macro-level behavior is determined by the
rules governing its micro-level elements. Contrast **top-down causality**. → [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Causal loop diagram** — a map of a system showing all constituent components and their
interactions, revealing the structure of its feedback loops. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md)

**Centrality** — a measure of how structurally important a node is in a network: by degree
(direct connections), closeness (short paths to everyone), betweenness (lies on many shortest
paths), or eigenvector (connected to other well-connected nodes). → [09](09-networks.md)

**Chaos (sensitive dependence)** — the property of a deterministic, nonlinear system whereby
arbitrarily small differences in initial conditions amplify into large differences in outcome,
making long-run behavior practically unpredictable (the "butterfly effect"). → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Closed system** — a system that resists incorporating new inputs across its boundary; more
strongly defined by the static properties of the boundary itself. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Cluster / Clique** — a subset of nodes in a network far more densely interconnected with each
other than with the rest of the network, often built on homophily. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Co-evolution ("dancing landscape")** — the dynamic in which multiple adaptive agents adapt to
*each other* simultaneously, so each agent's fitness landscape keeps shifting as the others move
across it. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Complexity (as a graded property)** — a system's degree of complexity along at least four
dimensions: number of elements, their diversity, their interconnectivity, and their capacity to
adapt. → [02](02-system-fundamentals.md), [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Conceptual models** — the structures, shaped by our paradigm, through which we define and
interpret every impression we receive; they shape what we see, which shapes what we do, which
shapes the world we create, which feeds back to shape our conceptual models. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Consumptive function** — a function where the resource input transfers its energy to the
system (which conserves it internally) while outputting entropy/waste to the environment. → [07](07-energy-entropy-efficiency.md)

**Control system** — see **Regulatory mechanism**.

**Cybernetics** — the area of systems theory that studies regulatory mechanisms; designed to
steer/guide a system toward the environmental parameters needed for homeostasis. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md)

**Degree** — the number of connections (edges) a node has in a network; the simplest measure of
its local connectivity. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Differentiation** — a developmental process defined by the proliferation of subsystems and
specialized elements within a system, increasing its capacity to respond to a greater diversity
of environmental states. Its necessary counterpart is **Integration**; imbalance between the two,
in either direction, produces negative synergy. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Diffusion / Contagion** — the process by which something spreads across a network; **simple
contagion** needs only one exposure, **complex contagion** needs reinforcement from multiple
sources or a threshold to be crossed. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Dissipative structure / Far-from-equilibrium** — organized, low-entropy structure sustained by
continuous energy throughput rather than by settling into passive equilibrium (Prigogine). → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Downward causation** — the higher/macro level of an emergent system exerting causal influence
back down on its own lower-level substrate; the criterion that distinguishes strong from weak
emergence. Contrast **Epiphenomenon** (pure upward causation). → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md), [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Eco-system thinking** — seeing every entity as interconnected with every other in a network of
mutual influence, with no privileged center. Contrast **Ego-system thinking**. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Edge (network)** — a connection between two nodes, directed or undirected, weighted or
unweighted. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Edge of chaos** — the narrow dynamic zone, between rigid order and chaotic disintegration, in
which self-organization is most productive: enough variation to keep adapting, enough coherence
to remain a functioning whole. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Ego-system thinking** — a self-centered view of the world in which connections are drawn only
loosely, radiating out from oneself, without connections drawn between the other entities
themselves. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Element** — the most basic level of the systems hierarchy: a part with no further constituent
components, described only by its properties (e.g. an electron). → [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Emergence** — a process whereby larger entities, patterns, or regularities arise through
interactions among simpler entities that do not themselves exhibit those properties; what results
is *qualitatively* different from the parts, not just quantitatively greater. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Encapsulation** — nesting one model of a system inside another (which may itself be nested
within a third, etc.), creating a hierarchical structure of representation. → [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Energetics** — the area of study concerned with energy across all physical systems (broader
than thermodynamics), closely related to systems theory. → [07](07-energy-entropy-efficiency.md)

**Entropy** — the incapacity to perform work; a measure of the degree of disorder within a
system; the opposite of energy/resource; loosely, "waste." → [07](07-energy-entropy-efficiency.md)

**Environment** — the sum total of other systems and input/output resources that a system
interacts with; everything outside a system's boundary; defined relative to the system's
functioning. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md), [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Epiphenomenon** — a phenomenon fully explainable by relations among lower-level features,
assumed to exert no independent causal effect back on them; the product of pure upward causation.
Contrast **Downward causation**. → [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Epistemological emergence** — emergence that is a limit of our knowledge/computing power (in
practice we can't derive the macro from the micro, though nothing prevents it in principle).
Contrast **Ontological emergence**. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Evolution (as a general process)** — change, variety, selection, and replication, generalized
past biology to markets, institutions, and technologies. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Externality** — an effect that lands on another agent or system without ever looping back to
its own source; unlike feedback, not self-correcting, and typically requires outside governance.
Contrast **Feedback loop**. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md)

**Feedback loop** — a channel or pathway formed by an effect returning to its cause, generating
either more (**positive**) or less/opposite (**negative**) of the same effect. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md)

**Fitness landscape** — a spatial model of evolutionary search, where each point is a possible
configuration and height represents fitness; search faces an explore-vs-exploit trade-off. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Flow** — the rate of change in a stock, measured over an interval of time. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md)

**Function** — a process that transforms energy or resources from one state to another, via
input, process, and output; the defining feature that distinguishes a system from a mere set. → [02](02-system-fundamentals.md)

**Geodesic** — the shortest path between two nodes in a network. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Holism** — the paradigm underlying Systems Thinking: understanding things within the context of
the whole environment they're part of, and how they are shaped by their nexus of relations.
Contrast **Reductionism**. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Homeostasis** — the state in which a system's internal variables are regulated to remain stable
and relatively constant despite changes in its external environment; one **attractor** among
several a system could settle into, not necessarily the default. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md), [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Homophily** — the tendency of similar nodes in a network to connect to each other ("birds of a
feather"), a common basis for clustering. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Hub** — a node with disproportionately high connectivity in a network, aggregating and
distributing flow both locally and globally. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Input** — elements captured and assembled at a system's boundary to be processed by its
function; systems can only process a specific range of inputs. → [02](02-system-fundamentals.md)

**Integration** — the process of recombining differentiated, specialized parts into a
coordinated, functioning whole; forms a dialectic with **Differentiation** — each enables the
other. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Integrative levels (theory of)** — the theory that units of matter are organized into levels of
increasing integration and complexity, describing the evolution from the inanimate to the animate
to the social world; higher levels are more complex, depend on and are made precarious by their
lower-level building blocks, and have progressively fewer instances. → [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Integrity** — the degree to which a system's parts are working together as an entirety, giving
the system a degree of autonomy. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Interference** — a destructive relation: prevention of a process or activity from being carried
out properly due to interaction between elements or systems, reducing the combined output below
the sum of the parts. Contrast **Synergy**. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Isolated system** — a system that does not interact with its surroundings in any way; more
restrictive than closed. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Iterated (repeated) game** — a game played repeatedly by the same agents, who can condition
their next move on the others' past behavior; unlike a one-shot game, this allows cooperative
strategies (e.g. **tit-for-tat**) to outperform purely exploitative ones and stabilize as the
long-run equilibrium. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Model** — an abstract, compact representation of a phenomenon, built by removing detail while
retaining what's essential; judged by usefulness, not by being "correct." → [11](11-models-and-modeling.md)

**Nash equilibrium** — a state in a game where no single agent can improve its own outcome by
unilaterally changing strategy, given every other agent's current strategy; not necessarily the
best *collective* outcome (mutual defection is a Nash equilibrium in the one-shot prisoner's
dilemma even though mutual cooperation would be better for both) — this is why **social
dilemmas** are stable traps rather than mere bad luck. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Negative feedback loop** — a feedback loop where linked elements change in opposite directions;
typically associated with stable, sustainable development. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md)

**Network effect** — a network's value grows disproportionately (often quadratically) with its
number of connected nodes (Metcalfe's law) — the diffusion dynamic viewed from the value side. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Node** — an entity, with properties, positioned within a network. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Ontological emergence** — emergence that claims the macro level is, in principle, irreducible
to the micro level — a fact about the world, independent of any observer's knowledge or
computational limits. Contrast **Epistemological emergence**. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Open system** — a system that interfaces and interacts with its environment across a permeable
boundary, exchanging materials, energy, information, or ideas. → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Order from noise / Order through fluctuations** — the principle that random perturbations are
the raw material self-organization amplifies into new order (von Foerster). → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Output** — energy or resources produced by a system's process that travel across its boundary
back to the environment. → [02](02-system-fundamentals.md)

**Paradigm** — the worldview underlying a theory's assumptions and methods; the foundation
shaping what we notice and how we build any theory. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Path (network)** — a sequence of edges connecting two nodes. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Percolation threshold** — the point below which nothing spreads system-wide across a network
and above which the same local rule produces a qualitatively different, system-wide outcome; the
network-diffusion counterpart of a phase transition. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Perspective-taking** — recognizing that any single observer, limited to one vantage point,
naturally mistakes their own partial view for the whole; a precondition for systems awareness. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Phase transition** — a rapid or accelerated period in a system's development on either side of
which the fundamental parameters describing the system change qualitatively; mechanistically, a
**bifurcation** triggered when feedback drives a system into a new attractor basin. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md), [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Positive feedback loop** — a feedback loop where linked elements change in the same direction;
typically associated with unstable, potentially exponential processes ("virtuous cycles") likely
to crash via **overshoot**. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md)

**Preferential attachment** — the mechanism ("rich get richer") by which new nodes in a network
preferentially connect to already well-connected ones, generating scale-free/power-law degree
distributions. → [09](09-networks.md)

**Process** — the series of actions performed on an input to transform it into an output; may be
cyclical, nested, or parallel rather than strictly linear. → [02](02-system-fundamentals.md)

**Process philosophy / Process ontology** — the view that reality is ceaseless becoming, and any
apparent stability (a structure, a boundary) is a temporary, self-sustaining pattern of process,
not a fixed underlying substance (Heraclitus's *panta rhei*). Contrast **Substance metaphysics**. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Productive function** — a function where a system performs work on an input resource, outputting
a resource of greater value than it took in. → [07](07-energy-entropy-efficiency.md)

**Protocol** — the specific channel, medium, and rules that let exchange succeed across a
permeable boundary (e.g. a shared language, a shared network protocol). → [03](03-boundary-and-environment.md)

**Punctuated equilibrium** — development that proceeds as long stable periods (negative feedback)
interrupted by short bursts of rapid, destabilizing change (positive feedback), rather than
smooth incremental growth. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Reductionism** — the process of breaking down or reducing systems to their constituent parts,
describing the whole primarily as the sum of these elements; historically anchored in the
**Mechanistic (Newtonian) paradigm**. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Reflexivity** — the bidirectional relationship between our subjective conceptual categories and
the world we perceive: mental models shape what we see, but what we see also reshapes the models. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Regulatory mechanism / Control system** — the mechanism by which a system monitors feedback and
acts to maintain homeostasis within its target environmental parameters; formally composed of a
**sensor**, **controller**, and **actuator**, and subject to **requisite variety** (Ashby's Law:
a regulator needs at least as many internal states as the states it must control). → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md)

**Relation** — a connection or interaction between two or more components, through which matter,
energy, information, or ideas are exchanged, binding the elements into interdependency. Not all
relations carry equal structural weight — see **Weak tie**. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Requisite variety** — see **Regulatory mechanism**.

**Resilience** — a system's capacity to absorb a disturbance and adapt into a new but still-viable
state. Contrast **Robustness** (resist and stay the same) and **Antifragility** (improve because
of the disorder). → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Resource** — a stored form of energy; an ordered structure that enables a system to perform
work. Opposite of **entropy**. → [07](07-energy-entropy-efficiency.md)

**Robustness (of a system's state)** — a system's capacity to resist a disturbance and remain in
its current state. See also **network robustness** ([09](09-networks.md)) for the structural
sense of the term. Contrast **Resilience**, **Antifragility**. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Schema** — a cognitive/conceptual framework used to classify, interpret, and predict, rather
than a fixed stimulus-response rule; what separates a simple control system from an adaptive
agent capable of learning. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Second-order cybernetics** — the recognition that an observer/regulator is part of the system
it observes and regulates, so that observation is itself a feedback loop capable of modifying the
observer's own models. → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Self-organization** — the process by which a system spontaneously develops coherent, system-
wide structure out of purely local interactions among its elements, with no centralized
controller. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md), [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Self-organized criticality** — a brittle, over-organized state in which a system is poised so
that a small, ordinary input can trigger a disproportionate cascade (e.g. a sandpile avalanche). → [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Set** — a group of objects that share no common function and exist independently of one
another; described completely by the sum of its elements' individual properties. Contrast
**System**. → [02](02-system-fundamentals.md)

**Simple / Complicated / Complex (typology)** — a classification by symmetry: simple systems have
many symmetries (few rules generate the pattern), complicated systems have none (must be
described part by part), complex systems interleave symmetry and asymmetry. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Small-world phenomenon** — the combination of high local clustering with surprisingly short
average path length across a network ("six degrees of separation"). → [09](09-networks.md)

**Social dilemma** — an interaction that is individually rational but collectively destructive
(e.g. the *tragedy of the commons*), typically arising from a zero-sum or externality-laden
relation. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md), [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)

**Strong emergence** — emergence involving genuine **downward causation**: the phenomenon cannot
be reduced to, predicted from, or traced back to the individual components even in principle, and
requires genuinely new descriptive vocabulary at the higher level (e.g. consciousness, quantum
entanglement). Contrast **Weak emergence**. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Stock** — any entity that accumulates or depletes over time; a variable measured as a quantity
at a point in time — it would still exist if time stopped, unlike a **flow**. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md)

**Substance metaphysics** — the view that reality is made of static "things" with properties
fixed at each instant, and process is secondary to that stable substance (Parmenides). Contrast
**Process philosophy**. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Subsystem** — a set of elements that makes up a system, which is itself a component of a larger
system. → [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Synergy** — a constructive relation: interaction or cooperation of two or more components
producing a combined effect greater than the sum of their separate effects, favored by
**positive-sum** relations. Contrast **Interference**. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Synthesis** — the foundational method of reasoning behind Systems Thinking: understanding an
entity through the context of its relations within the whole it is part of. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**System** — a composite entity of interconnected, interdependent elements arranged to perform
some collective function; distinguished from a set by the presence of relations and shared
function. → [02](02-system-fundamentals.md)

**System-of-systems** — the hierarchical level at which a system (itself made of subsystems) is
understood as one component within a still-larger system. → [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Systems awareness** — the reflexive recognition that how we see the world is shaped by our
conceptual models/paradigm, not given objectively; the necessary starting point of Systems
Thinking. → [01](01-worldview-and-paradigm.md)

**Systems dynamics** — the branch of systems theory that models how systems change over time via
internal feedback loops and time delays. → [06](06-dynamics-feedback-homeostasis.md)

**Systems efficiency** — the ratio between the energy input to a system and the energy it
outputs; note that what counts as "energy" vs. "entropy" is relative to context. → [07](07-energy-entropy-efficiency.md)

**Systems science** — the interdisciplinary application of systems theory across scientific
domains, emphasizing how domains fit together (e.g. system ecology, sociotechnical systems) over
siloed specialization. → [08](08-systems-science.md)

**Systems theory** — the formal, internally-consistent language (like mathematics) used to
describe systems of any kind, independent of any particular empirical subject matter. → [08](08-systems-science.md)

**Thermodynamics** — the area of study concerned with energy specifically in relation to heat (a
subset of the broader concern of energetics). → [07](07-energy-entropy-efficiency.md)

**Top-down causality** — the view that a system's micro-level elements are governed by rules
determined at the macro/whole-system level; one framing resolves this as the micro level
answering "how" (what's possible) and the macro level answering "why" (what's selected). Contrast
**Bottom-up causality**. → [05](05-hierarchy-and-abstraction.md)

**Weak emergence** — emergence involving only *upward* causation: the resulting phenomenon can,
in principle, be traced back to, predicted from, and computed from the individual elements, given
sufficient information (e.g. Conway's Game of Life) — it's just often too computationally
expensive to do so in practice. Contrast **Strong emergence**. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md)

**Weak tie** — a comparatively infrequent connection bridging two otherwise-separate clusters of
a network; disproportionately important for a system's diversity and integration despite its low
individual weight (Granovetter). → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md), [09](09-networks.md)

**Zero-sum / Positive-sum game** — a formal characterization of a relation: zero-sum (one side's
gain is exactly the other's loss) favors interference; positive-sum (both sides can gain) favors
synergy. → [04](04-relations-synergy-emergence.md), [10](10-complex-adaptive-systems.md)
